  • 5–6 t-shirts / lightweight shirts
  • 2–3 pairs of lightweight pants or chinos
  • 2–3 pairs of shorts for dorm and casual weekends
  • 1–2 lightweight long-sleeve layers or zip hoodie
  • 7–8 pairs of socks — feet are visible constantly in shoes-off culture

Business Casual (Company Visits)

  • 2 collared dress shirts — mandatory for Japanese company visits
  • 1 pair of dress pants or smart chinos — not jeans for company visits
  • Smart shoes or clean leather sneakers — slip-on preferred
  • Belt to match dress pants

Rain & Rainy Season (June – mid-July)

  • Lightweight packable rain jacket — tsuyu rainy season starts early June. Non-negotiable.
  • Compact travel umbrella
  • 2–3 quick-dry shirts — clothes dry slowly in rainy season humidity

Footwear

  • Comfortable walking sneakers
  • Easy slip-on shoes or sandals — dorm, onsen, shoes-off locations
  • Dedicated gym-only athletic shoes — APU requires indoor-only shoes. Sizes above US 11 very hard to find in Beppu.
  • Flip flops — communal shower areas and AP House floors

Onsen & Farm Stay

  • Old clothes you don't mind getting dirty for the farm stay weekend
  • Swimwear — for any pool use (NOT for traditional onsen)

Solo Travel Week (Jul 19–26)

  • 2–3 clean outfits for travel week — July is 30°C+ and very humid in Tokyo/Kyoto/Hiroshima
  • Small daypack or crossbody bag — for day trips, water bottle, umbrella

Skip heavy jeans and bulky sweaters — Beppu summer is hot and humid. Pack light fabrics.

💳 Money Tips
Always pay in YEN — never USD. When a terminal asks "Pay in USD or JPY?" — always choose JPY. Paying in USD adds a hidden 3–6% dynamic currency conversion markup.
  • Visa and Mastercard accepted almost everywhere that takes cards
  • American Express works at hotels, department stores, and larger restaurants
  • Many small restaurants, shrines, temples, and rural shops are cash only
  • 7-Eleven (7-Bank) and Lawson ATMs reliably accept US cards — English menus
  • Coins are high-value — ¥500 ≈ $3.25, ¥100 ≈ $0.65. Use them constantly.
  • Coins cannot be exchanged back to USD — spend them before flying home
  • Show passport at larger stores for 8–10% tax refund on purchases over ¥5,000
  • Banks in Japan close at 3:00pm and are closed on weekends

📋 Cultural Essentials
  • No tipping — ever. Restaurants, taxis, hotels.
  • Shoes off in dorm rooms, tatami areas, many restaurants.
  • Trains are silent. No calls. Manner mode (マナーモード).
  • Cars drive on the LEFT. Look right before stepping off curbs.
  • Coins are high-value. ¥500 = ~$3.25. Use them constantly.
  • Tax-free shopping. Show passport for 8–10% refund over ¥5,000.
  • Onsen: Shower first. No swimwear. No towels in the water.
  • Trash cans are rare — carry a small bag for wrappers.
