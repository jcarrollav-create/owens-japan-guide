🚇 IC Cards — The Most Important Thing
Do First

An IC card (Suica or ICOCA) is a rechargeable transit card that works on virtually every train, subway, and bus in Japan. It also pays at convenience stores, vending machines, and coin lockers. Get one at Haneda Airport on arrival and never look back.

IC cards work in Fukuoka (Nimoca/Hayakaken), Beppu local buses, and all major cities. Suica is universally accepted everywhere. Load ¥3,000–5,000 to start. Keep ¥500–1,000 loaded at all times — gates will reject you on empty.
🚄 Shinkansen (Bullet Train)
Intercity

The shinkansen connects major cities at 200–300 km/h. It's the backbone of long-distance Japan travel — punctual to the minute, comfortable, and surprisingly straightforward once you understand the system.

  • Book at smart-ex.jp — Japan's official Shinkansen booking site. English interface, credit card, e-ticket on your phone. Best prices and reserved seats. smart-ex.jp/en
  • Or buy at the station — JR ticket machines (green machines) have English menus. Select your destination, choose unreserved (自由席) for flexibility or reserved (指定席) to guarantee a seat.
  • Unreserved vs reserved — Reserved costs ¥500–1,000 more but guarantees your seat. Unreserved is fine outside of Friday evenings and holiday weekends.
  • Platforms — Follow the 新幹線 (Shinkansen) signs. Check your train name (e.g. Sakura, Kodama, Hikari, Nozomi) and car/seat number on your ticket.
  • You do NOT need a JR Pass for Owen's travel route. Individual tickets via smart-ex.jp are cheaper. JR Pass only makes sense for very extensive nationwide travel.

🚆 Local Trains & Subways

Every city has its own subway and local rail network. All use the same IC card tap-in/tap-out system. Use Google Maps or Navitime for directions — both show exact train numbers, platforms, and which car to board.

  • Fukuoka Subway — 3 lines covering the city. Airport Line goes directly to Fukuoka Airport. Tap Suica or buy tickets from machines. subway.city.fukuoka.lg.jp
  • Beppu — no subway. Use local buses (Kamenoi Bus) or taxis. APU has a campus shuttle between the hilltop campus and Beppu Station. IC card works on Kamenoi buses.
  • Tokyo Metro / Toei — Tokyo's subway system. Two operators but Suica works on both. Very frequent — trains every 2–5 minutes most of the day. tokyometro.jp/en
  • Kyoto — buses. Kyoto's main sightseeing network is buses, not subway. ¥230 flat fare, IC card works. Buy a ¥700 day pass at the bus terminal for unlimited rides.
  • Hiroshima — trams (streetcars). The historic tram network connects the station to Peace Park and the ferry terminal. ¥190 flat fare, IC card works.
  • Navitime Japan — best app for trains. Shows exact platform, car number, transfer instructions, and real-time delays. Download before arriving. navitime.co.jp
🚌 Buses
  • Long-distance highway buses — Cheap alternative to Shinkansen. Overnight buses save hotel costs. Book at bushikaku.net/en or willer.co.jp/en. Fukuoka → Osaka overnight is about ¥4,000–6,000.
  • Local city buses — Pay with IC card (tap when boarding, tap when exiting) or exact cash. Most city buses announce stops in English in major cities.
  • Beppu — Kamenoi Bus — The main local bus company in Beppu. IC card accepted. Runs between APU, Beppu Station, and the onsens. Ask the program directors for the relevant bus numbers from campus.
  • Airport buses — Most airports have fixed-fare limousine bus services to city hotels. Cheaper than taxis for solo travelers, good when you have luggage. No reservation needed usually — buy at the bus counter on arrival.
🚕 Taxis
  • Hail on the street — Look for 空車 (kūsha = vacant) sign in the windshield, usually illuminated in red/green. Wave to stop. No need to open the door — rear doors open automatically.
  • No tipping — ever. Tipping in Japan is considered rude. Pay the meter and leave.
  • Payment — IC cards now accepted in most taxis in major cities. Cash always works. Credit card is hit or miss — confirm before getting in.
  • Cost — Base fare ¥500–730, then ¥80–90 per 300m. Short 10-minute rides can easily reach ¥1,200–1,800. Tokyo taxis are among the most expensive in the world.
  • Show your destination — Have it written in Japanese or pull up Google Maps and show the driver. Many drivers speak little English but will follow map directions.
At Fukuoka Airport, look for the fixed-fare taxi desk in arrivals. A fixed fare to Hakata/Tenjin is cheaper than running the meter and you know the price upfront.
📱 Rideshare Apps
Japan rideshare ≠ Uber in the US. All apps dispatch licensed taxi drivers — not private cars. You'll get a proper taxi with a professional driver.
  • GO App — The dominant taxi app in Japan. Works in Fukuoka, Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto, and most cities. English interface. Download before arriving. go.mo-t.com/en
  • Uber Japan — Limited coverage. Available in Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, and a few others. More expensive than GO in most cases. Good as backup. uber.com
  • DiDi Japan — Available in major cities. Similar to GO. Download at didimobility.co.jp/en
  • In Beppu — App-based services have limited coverage. Use street taxis or call a taxi from your hotel. Beppu is small enough that most destinations are ¥600–1,200 away.

💳 Money Tips
Always pay in YEN — never USD. When a terminal asks "Pay in USD or JPY?" — always choose JPY. Paying in USD adds a hidden 3–6% dynamic currency conversion markup.
  • Visa and Mastercard accepted almost everywhere that takes cards
  • American Express works at hotels, department stores, and larger restaurants
  • Many small restaurants, shrines, temples, and rural shops are cash only
  • 7-Eleven (7-Bank) and Lawson ATMs reliably accept US cards — English menus
  • Coins are high-value — ¥500 ≈ $3.25, ¥100 ≈ $0.65. Use them constantly.
  • Coins cannot be exchanged back to USD — spend them before flying home
  • Show passport at larger stores for 8–10% tax refund on purchases over ¥5,000
  • Banks in Japan close at 3:00pm and are closed on weekends

📋 Cultural Essentials
  • No tipping — ever. Restaurants, taxis, hotels.
  • Shoes off in dorm rooms, tatami areas, many restaurants.
  • Trains are silent. No calls. Manner mode (マナーモード).
  • Cars drive on the LEFT. Look right before stepping off curbs.
  • Coins are high-value. ¥500 = ~$3.25. Use them constantly.
  • Tax-free shopping. Show passport for 8–10% refund over ¥5,000.
  • Onsen: Shower first. No swimwear. No towels in the water.
  • Trash cans are rare — carry a small bag for wrappers.
